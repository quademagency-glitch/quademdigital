## Section 01: The list you want does not exist

Lead tools work off standard boxes. Industry, job title, company size, location. Tick the
boxes, get your list. That is fine if your customer fits in a box.

Good customers never do.

**Two column comparison. Left is muted, right is accented.**

| What a lead tool lets you ask for | What you actually want |
|---|---|
| Dentists in Manchester | Dentists who just opened a second branch |
| Companies with 10 to 50 staff | Wedding venues with no video on their website |
| Anyone with "Marketing Manager" in their title | Online shops doing well but on a slow, dated platform |
| Businesses in hospitality | Gyms paying for Facebook ads with almost no Instagram following |
| Firms started after 2018 | Factories whose website has not been touched in ten years |

There is no box for anything in the right hand column. You cannot buy that list.

But every one of those can be worked out from information anybody can see. Somebody just
has to go and look, at scale, and know what they are looking for. That is the whole
service. Not a secret database. Looking properly, every month, at the things other people
cannot be bothered to check.

## Section 02: What arrives

**Three tiles.**

**The list**
A spreadsheet of businesses matching your definition. Company name, contact name where it
can be found, a checked email address, phone number, website and town.

**The reason**
One line per business saying why it is on the list this month. "Opened a second site in
June." "No mobile version of their site." That line is what gets your email answered.

**The clean-up**
Nobody appears twice. Anyone already your customer is removed. Anyone who said no is
removed permanently. Anything that bounced last month is gone.

**Pull quote under the tiles**
The third one sounds like housekeeping. It is the reason clients stay. Everyone who has
bought leads before has been burned by paying twice for the same dead names.

## Section 03: How it works

**Numbered, three steps.**

**One. We work out who your customer actually is.**
A conversation, then a written definition you sign off. This is the part that takes real
thought, and it is why the first month costs more than the ones after it. If the
definition is vague, everything downstream is vague.

**Two. I go and find them.**
Public information, read at scale, filtered against your definition, then checked by hand
before anything reaches you. The checking is not optional and it is not automated away.

**Three. It happens again next month.**
People change jobs. Businesses close. Websites get rebuilt. A list handed over six months
ago is partly wrong today, which is why this is a monthly service and not a file you buy
once.

## Section 06: What this is not

**Restraint section. This is the one that earns trust, do not cut it for length.**

**It is not software.** You are not buying a login and you are not being asked to run
anything. If you wanted a tool you would already have bought one, and it would be sitting
unused.

**It is not a database.** Nothing here is scraped from a list somebody else is also
selling. That is the point, and it is also why it costs more than a seat on a platform.

**It is not instant.** The first month is slow because the definition has to be right.
Months two onward are quick.

**It is not unconditional.** You get a loud voice pointed at real people, and my name is
attached to how you use it. The contract says unsubscribes are honoured, no lists bought
from anywhere else are mixed in, and nobody pretends to be someone they are not. If that
is a problem, this is not the right service.

## Section 08: Questions

**Render as a real FAQ with `FAQPage` schema. AI answer engines quote these directly, so
each answer has to stand alone without the rest of the page.**

**How is this different from a lead database?**
A database lets you filter on standard fields such as industry, job title and company
size. Fieldwork starts from a description of your customer that no database has a field
for, such as a business that recently opened a second location or one whose website has no
mobile version, and finds those businesses by reading public information about them.

**How many businesses do I get?**
It depends on how narrow your definition is. A tight definition in a small market might
return sixty a month and be worth more than six hundred loose ones. The number is agreed
before you sign, not promised in advance.

**Are the email addresses verified?**
Yes. Every address is checked before delivery, and anything that bounced the previous
month is removed from the next list.

**Can I buy a single list instead of subscribing?**
No. A list decays from the day it is made, and a one off sale means you get the blame for
data that was accurate when it left. This is monthly or not at all.

**Who owns the data?**
You do. It is your list, delivered to you, and you can keep it if you stop.

**Do you send the emails as well?**
Only on the done for you tier, and only under the conduct rules in the contract.
