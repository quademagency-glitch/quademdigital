# Electronic signing, 4 October 2026

Ernest asked for a way to upload a document in the CMS and send it for
electronic signature. His documents already have a signature section, so the
system reads that section and lets each person sign in their own place, rather
than asking him to draw boxes on every PDF.

## How it works

1. **CMS, Documents to sign** (CRM & Sales). Upload a PDF, list who signs it
   (name, email, and optionally role, company and job title), save.
2. On save the PDF's signature section is read (`cms/src/lib/signing/detect.ts`)
   and each place is matched to a signer (`assign.ts`). The panel at the top of
   the request lists every place with a dropdown to change the match.
3. **Send**. Each signer gets a private link to `quademdigital.com/sign/<token>/`
   by email. The panel also has "Copy link" for sending by WhatsApp.
4. The signer reads the document on the page, taps each yellow box, draws or
   types their signature once (and initials once, if the document asks), ticks
   the consent box and signs. Name, date and job title places fill themselves.
5. When the last person signs, the PDF is stamped (`stamp.ts`): every signature,
   initials, name and date goes into its place, a reference line goes in the
   bottom margin of every page, and a **signing certificate** is added at the
   end. Everyone, Ernest included, is emailed the signed copy as an attachment.
   It is also kept in **Signed documents**, which nobody can edit or delete.

Options per request: ask for a code (a six-digit code emailed when the link is
opened, so a forwarded link is useless), sign one after another, how long the
link works (30 days), and automatic reminders (every 3 days, at most three).
Ernest can send a reminder or withdraw a request at any time from the panel. A
signer can decline, with a reason, which ends the request and tells everyone.

## Editing the document: blanks, moving and adding places

Added the same day, after Ernest asked to edit "the blank spaces". Below the
list of signers, a request now shows **The document**: every page with every
place laid over it (`cms/src/components/SigningPlacesEditor.tsx`).

- **Blanks you fill in.** Underscore blanks outside a signature block ("This
  Agreement is entered into on ____", "Contact person: ____") are found on
  upload and listed at the top with a box to type into. What Ernest types is
  shown to signers as part of the page and printed into the signed copy. One
  left empty stays blank.
- **Blanks a signer fills in.** An underscore blank inside a signature block
  ("Address: ____" under a party) belongs to that party's signer, who types it
  on the signing page. It is required unless Ernest unticks "They must fill it
  in". A signer can only ever write into their own blanks.
- **Click a place** to change what it is, who fills it, or what goes in it.
  **Drag** it to move it, drag its **corner** to resize it, arrow keys nudge
  it. **Delete** removes it, which is how an unwanted witness line goes.
  **Add a place** puts a new signature, initials, name, date, job title or text
  blank wherever Ernest clicks, for anyone on the list.

Every change saves as it is made, through `POST /api/signature-requests/:id/places`,
which checks each place (`cleanPlaces` in `lib/signing/places.ts`) and refuses
anything once the request has been sent. Who fills a place is written in its
`party`, described at the top of `places.ts`: a detected block, `signer:<id>`
for one chosen by hand, `sender` for Ernest's own blanks, or `none`.

Typed text is never cut short in the signed copy: it shrinks to fit, wraps when
the place is tall enough, and at the smallest size carries on below the place
rather than ending in an ellipsis.

Checked before it shipped: the editor in a stand-in admin page in both themes
(type a blank, reassign, drag, resize, nudge, add, delete; every save passed
`cleanPlaces`), the signing page against a stand-in CMS on a phone (blanks
counted towards Finish, an empty required blank refused, a write into someone
else's blank ignored), and a stamped copy with long and short entries. The
three real agreements kept exactly the places they had before; Omek and
Citywide gained their contact and date blanks, labelled correctly.

## Ernest changing it while he signs

Ernest asked to make his changes in the CMS first, and then still be able to
change things when he signs, with everyone else limited to their own places.
So when the signer is the person who sent the request (their email is the
sender's), the signing page lets them:

- change any blank that is theirs to fill before sending, or that nobody fills,
  including one left empty (shown with a blue dashed outline);
- add text anywhere on a page ("add text" in the note at the top, then tap).

Only while **nobody else has signed**: once anyone has, the page offers
nothing to change and the CMS refuses a change sent anyway (409, `locked`),
because it would alter what that person signed. A change is saved with the
signature, in the request's places, and logged as "Changed while signing by
...". The time of the last change is the document's **version**: every signing
page sends back the version it showed, and one opened before a change is told
to refresh rather than sign the old wording (409, `changed`). Tested with the
real `flow.ts` against an in-memory stand-in for Payload: Ernest changed two
blanks and added a note; Ama, on a page opened earlier, was stopped; after a
refresh she saw the change and signed; the signed copy carried all of it.

## Found on the second pass

- **Every fingerprint was the fingerprint of an empty file.** pdf.js takes over
  the buffer it is given and leaves it empty, and the detector was handed the
  hook's own bytes, which were hashed afterwards. Finishing compares the stored
  PDF with that fingerprint, so no request could ever have completed. The
  detector now works on a copy and the hook hashes first.
- **Blanks drawn as a line** ("Address:" then a rule, page 1 of Charles's
  agreement) are now found, when the words before the line end in a colon and
  nothing is written on it.
- **A blank outside the signature section goes to whoever its box names**
  ("THE TRAINEE · Charles Ohanu" over that address), when exactly one signer
  fits; otherwise it is Ernest's. Omek's and Citywide's client contact details
  go to the client; their agreement and start dates stay with Ernest.

## Sending it again after withdrawing

A request that was withdrawn, declined or expired shows **Send it again**.
It makes a new draft (`copyRequest` in `flow.ts`, `POST /api/signature-requests/:id/copy`)
with the same PDF, signers, message, settings and every place, including what
Ernest moved, added or typed, and opens it to check and send. Nothing is
emailed until he presses Send on the copy. The old request stays as the record,
and both histories say where the copy came from and went to.

Every array row id is unique across all requests, so signers, parties and
places get new ids, and each matched party and each `signer:<id>` place is
pointed at the new signer ids. Creating the draft runs the upload hook, which
reads the PDF afresh; the copied places then replace what it found.

Tested with the real `flow.ts`: a copy of a still-out request is refused;
after withdrawing, the copy is a draft with the same file bytes, people and
all twelve places, typed values kept, Ama's hand-given blank still hers under
her new id, no email sent; sending the copy works, the old link says the
document was withdrawn, and the new link shows her places.

## Correcting a mistyped email after sending

**Wrong email address?** appears on a request while it is out for signing.
Ernest picks someone who has not signed yet, types the right address and
presses **Save and send a new link** (`changeSignerEmail` in `flow.ts`,
`POST /api/signature-requests/:id/change-email`). Nothing restarts and nobody
who has signed is asked again. The person's old link stops working, because it
went to an address that may be someone else's; anything that address did (an
opening, a code) is cleared; a new link goes to the corrected address, or, if
they are still waiting their turn, goes there when it comes. Someone who has
signed cannot have their address changed, since it is part of the record. The
correction is in the history, so the certificate shows it, old address
included.

Tested with the real `flow.ts`: after Ernest signed, Ama's address was put
right; one email went, to the new address only; her old link was refused, the
new one opened, she signed and the request completed with the corrected
address on the certificate. With signing in order, correcting her address
while she waited sent nothing until Ernest signed, then sent her link to the
new address.

## Every kind of blank

Ernest asked for the document to be read for any blank space that needs
filling. On upload, and again when he presses **Find blanks again** in the
editor (which adds only what no place already covers, and touches nothing he
changed), the detector now finds:

| Kind | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Underscores | `Name: ________` (also outside a signature block now) |
| Dots | `Phone: ..........`, `Email: ………` (a row of dots ending in a page number is a contents line, and skipped) |
| A drawn line | with its words beside it (`Dated this ____ day of ____`, no colon needed), above it, or just below it (`Print name of witness`) |
| A placeholder | `[Client name]`, `[insert date]`, `[●]`, `{{fee}}`, `<<Address>>`, `«Name»`; what is typed covers the placeholder, on screen and in the signed copy. `[see clause 4]` is not one. |
| An empty box | an outlined box or table cell with nothing in it, captioned by the text above or beside it |
| Empty labels | `Bank name:` `Account number:` `Branch:` stacked with nothing after them; a lone `Notes:` over a paragraph is a heading, and skipped |

Tables are the main source of false blanks, because each row's divider is
drawn cell by cell under the cell's text. Divider pieces that meet end to end,
and identical pieces stacked one per row, are not blank lines. Section
headings set larger than the body, and the rule beside them, are not either.
Checked on a test page with every kind plus those traps (17 found, no trap
taken) and on the three real agreements, where every earlier place is
unchanged and the only additions are real blanks, including Charles's address
in the parties clause on page 2 ("Charles Ohanu, of ______, Nigeria").

A blank goes to a signer when the words just before it name them ("Charles
Ohanu, of ____") or its box does; one pointing at the sender, compared by
email, or at nobody clearly, is the sender's to fill before sending.

## What the detector reads

Tested against the three real agreements, with the result checked by eye:

| Document | Layout | Found |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Omek service agreement | "Signature" label below a drawn line, two columns (SERVICE PROVIDER, CLIENT) | both signatures, the client's name, both dates |
| Citywide agreement | inline "Signature: ____", "Date: ____", plus a witness block per party | both signatures, dates, the client's name and position, both witnesses' names and signatures |
| Charles's Business Development Agreement | letter-spaced labels above an empty space, witness blocks, "Initials: Company ____ Trainee ____" on every page | both signatures, both dates, both witnesses, initials on pages 2 to 7 |

Signature lines are drawn shapes, not text, so the labels are what is read, and
each box is snapped to the nearest drawn line in its column. Whose column it is
comes from the words around it ("CLIENT", "For Citywide Property Services
Ltd", "THE TRAINEE · Charles Ohanu"). A place nobody is matched to stays blank;
a signer with no place in the document signs on the certificate page instead.

## The certificate

The last page records the reference, the document's SHA-256 fingerprint as
sent, when it was sent and completed, each signer's signature, email, time,
IP address and device, how they were identified (the email link, and the code
if one was asked for), and the full history. The fingerprint lets anyone check
the signed copy came from exactly the document that was sent.

## Decisions worth keeping

- **Each signer's progress is its own record** (`signing-sessions`), not a row
  inside the request, so two people signing in the same second cannot
  overwrite each other's signature. Finishing is guarded by one conditional
  `UPDATE ... WHERE status = 'out'`, so it runs exactly once.
- **Links are stored as a hash** (for lookup) and sealed with a key derived from
  `PAYLOAD_SECRET` (so Ernest can copy them). A copy of the database is not a
  set of working links.
- **"Opened" is recorded by the page's own script**, not when the website
  renders it. Gmail, Outlook and WhatsApp fetch every link to preview it, and
  the certificate should not say Microsoft's link scanner opened the contract.
- **The signing page loads nothing from elsewhere.** It does not use
  BaseLayout, because Tag Manager, Analytics, Ahrefs, Metricool and the chat
  widget would each record the private link. pdf.js is self-hosted in
  `public/vendor/pdfjs`; the typed signature uses Dancing Script, self-hosted.
- **Signing links use `quademdigital.com`, not `ASTRO_SITE_URL`**, which is
  `http://localhost:4321` on Railway. `SIGNING_SITE_URL` overrides it for tests.
- **`/sign` is never cached** at the edge (`src/middleware.ts`).
- **Finishing runs after the signer gets their answer**, because stamping and
  emailing three copies can outlast the website's request time limit. The
  five-minute job (`signingRounds`) finishes anything a restart cut short,
  expires old requests and sends the reminders.
- **Once sent, nothing can change** except the client and team member links. A
  mistake is fixed by withdrawing and sending a new request. A completed
  request cannot be deleted.
- Files go in the private documents bucket under their own prefixes
  (`signing/`, `signed/`), because the bucket is shared and Payload only keeps
  filenames unique within one collection.

## Found while building it

- `ASTRO_SITE_URL` on Railway is localhost: every signer would have been sent a
  link to `http://localhost:4321`.
- The generated migration's `down()` failed: it dropped the lock-table
  constraints by name after `DROP TABLE ... CASCADE` had already removed them.
  Caught by `cms/scripts/rehearse-migration.mjs`, new, which runs up, down and
  up again against production inside a transaction that is always rolled back.
- `signing_sessions.request_id` came out `NOT NULL` with `ON DELETE SET NULL`,
  so deleting a withdrawn request would have failed. It is `CASCADE`.
- `migrate:create` wrote a macOS `._` sidecar into `src/migrations/index.ts` as
  if it were a migration. The Docker build excludes `._` files, so production
  would have failed to build. Caught by building the CMS locally.
- pnpm refuses to update the lockfile without deleting and reinstalling
  `node_modules` on this drive. The lockfile was generated in a scratch copy,
  merged so that nothing was removed (pnpm 9 drops the `libc` lines a newer
  pnpm wrote), and checked with `pnpm@9.15.4 install --frozen-lockfile`, the
  Docker build's own command.
