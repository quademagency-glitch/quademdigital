# Getting testimonials

The site has zero testimonials. That is the largest credibility gap left on
quademdigital.com, and it is the one thing on the list I cannot do for you:
the quotes have to come from real clients, in their words.

Everything else is ready. `TestimonialsSection.astro` hides itself completely
while there are none, so the site does not look broken in the meantime, and
`published` defaults to false in the CMS, so nothing appears until you tick it.

## Who to ask

Ask the people you actually did paid work for. Your own projects (QuadERP,
QuadBrand, the Quadem brand identity) do not count and would read as padding.

Start with the client whose result you can point at. Omek is the obvious first
ask: 151 products live on a storefront you rebuilt from a badly built
WooCommerce site.

## Why ask this way

Most people freeze when asked to "write a testimonial", because it sounds like
homework and they do not know what you want. Three specific questions turn it
into a reply they can write in two minutes. Offering to tidy the wording and
send it back removes the last excuse, and it is honest: you are editing for
grammar, not putting words in their mouth.

Never write the quote yourself and ask them to approve it. It reads as fake to
the reader, and it is.

## Email

> **Subject:** A quick favour, if the work was useful
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I am putting a few client stories on my site and I would like to include
> [project].
>
> Would you be up for two or three sentences about working with me? It does
> not need to be polished. If it helps, answer whichever of these is easiest:
>
> - What was the problem before we started?
> - What is different now?
> - Would you recommend me to someone else, and why?
>
> Reply to this email and I will tidy up the wording, then send it back for
> your approval before anything goes live. Nothing gets published without you
> seeing it first.
>
> If you would rather not, that is completely fine and it changes nothing
> between us.
>
> Thanks,
> Ernest
> Quadem Digital

## WhatsApp

Most Ghanaian business runs here, and a voice note is easier than typing.

> Hi [Name], quick one. I am adding a few client stories to my site and would
> love to include [project]. Could you send me two or three sentences on what
> the work did for you? A voice note is fine, I will write it up and send it
> back for your approval before it goes anywhere. No stress at all if you would
> rather not.

## When a reply comes in

1. Tidy grammar only. Keep their voice, keep it specific, leave the numbers
   they gave you.
2. Send it back and wait for a yes in writing.
3. Add it in the CMS under Testimonials: name, role, quote, stars, and an
   avatar if they are happy to give a photo.
4. Tick **Published**. The section appears on its own once at least one is
   published.

A specific quote beats a glowing one. "He moved 151 products across without
losing a single order" is worth more than "great to work with".
