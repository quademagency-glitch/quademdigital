Quadem Digital · New service line

Sell them the list, not the effort

Every agency in the world sells work: we'll run your ads, we'll post for you, we'll send the emails. This sells something else, a machine that finds a client's customers every month, built only for them, that nobody else can buy off a shelf. It is the most profitable thing on your menu and the hardest for anyone to copy.

01

What it actually is

A client tells you exactly who their best customer is. You go and find every single one of them, with a name, an email, a phone number, and a reason they need calling this month. Then you do it again next month.

That's it. No mystery. The tool you already have does most of the work. It reads public information about businesses and works out which ones fit.

What makes it worth thousands rather than hundreds is that it's built for one client, around one definition of a customer. It isn't a login to a database everybody else already has.

02

Why nobody else can sell this

The big lead tools work off standard boxes. Industry. Job title. Company size. Tick the boxes, get your list. That's fine if your customer fits in a box.

The good customers never do. Here's the difference, and it's the whole business:

What a normal lead tool lets you ask for
  • Dentists in Manchester
  • Companies with 10 to 50 staff
  • Anyone with "Marketing Manager" in their title
  • Businesses in the hospitality industry
  • Firms started after 2018
What the client actually wants
  • Dentists who just opened a second branch
  • Wedding venues with no video on their website
  • Online shops doing well but on a slow, dated platform
  • Gyms paying for Facebook ads with almost no Instagram following
  • Factories whose website clearly hasn't been touched in ten years

There is no box for any of those. You cannot buy that list. But every one of them can be worked out from information anybody can see. You just have to go and look, at scale, and know what you're looking for.

That's the whole product. Not a secret database. Looking properly, every month, at things other people can't be bothered to check.

03

Who buys it

One test, and it's simple: is their customer hard to find?

Good fit

  • Suppliers selling one specialist thing to one specialist buyer
  • Companies selling equipment or machinery
  • Recruiters who work in one narrow field
  • Commercial builders and trade contractors
  • Software companies chasing one industry
  • Other agencies, who need leads too and will pay well

Walk away

  • "Our customer is basically anyone": nothing to build around
  • Shops selling to the public rather than to businesses
  • Anyone who wants a one-off list and no ongoing service
  • Anyone who sounds like they'll use it to spam people
  • Clients who won't tell you who their best customers actually are

The first question on every call: "Tell me about the last five customers you were really glad to get." If they can answer that clearly, you can build this. If they can't, they don't have a lead problem. They have a positioning problem, and that's a different sale.

04

Sell what comes out, not the machine

This is the most important decision in the whole offer, so don't skip it.

If you sell them a system, they have to run it. Most small businesses won't. It sits unused, they can't see what they're paying for, they cancel, and they tell people it didn't work.

So sell the output instead:

"Every month we send you 300 businesses that match exactly what you asked for. Each one comes with a real email address, a phone number, and a note explaining why they need you right now."

Same machine underneath. Much easier to sell, because they can picture it. Much easier to keep, because the day they stop paying, the leads stop arriving.

You can always offer them the keys later, at a price. Most won't want them.

05

What lands in their inbox

Be specific about this on every call. Vagueness is what kills the sale.

The list

A spreadsheet of businesses that match their definition. Company name, contact name where we can get it, checked email address, phone number, website, town.

The reason

One line per business saying why they're on the list this month. "Opened a second site in June." "No mobile version of their site." That line is what makes the client's own email get answered.

The clean-up

Nobody appears twice. Anyone already a customer is removed. Anyone who said no is removed, permanently. Anything that bounced last month is gone.

That third one sounds boring and it's the reason they stay. Every client who has bought leads before has been burned by getting the same dead names twice.

07

Three things that will bite you

It goes stale. People change jobs, businesses close, websites get rebuilt. A list you handed over six months ago is partly wrong today. If you sell it once and walk away, it rots and they blame you. Monthly or don't do it.

It's work, not software. Every client's niche is different, so you can't build it once and sell the same thing fifty times, at least not at first. Price it like a service you deliver, not an app you own. After five or six clients you'll see the repeating parts and it gets faster.

You're handing someone a loud voice. They will email strangers with what you give them. If they behave badly, your name is attached. Put the rules in the contract: no buying lists from anywhere else, unsubscribe honoured, no pretending to be someone they're not. Be willing to walk.

08

What to say on the call

Don't explain how it works. Nobody buys plumbing. Ask about their customers and let them talk themselves into it.

You

Tell me about the last five customers you were really glad to get. What did they have in common?

Them

…they were all mid-sized, all just expanding, all a bit behind on their marketing…

You

Right. And how do you find more of those at the moment?

Them

Referrals, mostly. Bit of LinkedIn. It's slow.

You

So you know exactly who you want and you're waiting for them to turn up. Here's what I do. I go and find every business in the country that matches what you just described, including the bit about expanding, which no lead tool on the market can search for. You get 300 of them a month, with the email, the phone number, and one line on why each one needs you right now.

You

I built it for my own agency first. It's how I found you.

That last line is the strongest sentence in the whole pitch. Make sure it's true before you use it.

09

The order to do this in

  1. Use it on yourself. Run it for Quadem. Win two or three international clients with it. Nothing else counts until this is done.
  2. Write down what happened. How many businesses found, how many replied, how many became clients. Real numbers from your own pipeline. That's your proof.
  3. Sell it to one agency. Agencies understand the value instantly and pay without much explaining. Do the first one cheap in exchange for permission to name them.
  4. Then sell it properly. Full price, to the specialist suppliers and equipment sellers. By now you have two case studies and a script that works.
  5. Notice what repeats. After five or six, the same pieces keep coming up. That's when it stops being a project each time and starts being a product.

Step one is not optional. Selling this before it has worked for you is selling an idea, and it shows. Selling it after is selling the thing that got you the client you're talking to.

10

The name: Fieldwork

"Custom lead generation system" sounds like everybody else. A name makes it a thing you own and a thing people repeat to each other. Decided 22 August 2026.

Fieldwork

Chosen. Names the actual difference between this and a lead tool: somebody went out and looked, at scale, every month. Extends into how the work is described on calls, in "this month's fieldwork" and the "field note" attached to each business.

Signal

Rejected. The word belongs to the messaging app. It cannot be ranked for, and it is misheard on calls.

Nightshift, The List

Rejected. Nightshift says when the work happens but not what arrives. The List is clear but unownable, hard to search for, and reads thin against a four figure monthly fee.

Fieldwork it is. It describes the actual difference between this and every other lead list, which is that somebody looked properly rather than filling in a form.

One caveat, checked on 22 August 2026. The word is being registered heavily right now. In .com, fieldwork.com, getfieldwork.com, fieldworkhq.com, thefieldwork.com, tryfieldwork.com, runfieldwork.com, fieldworkleads.com and fieldworkdigital.com are all taken, and five of those were registered in the last twelve months. fieldwork.agency, fieldwork.services, fieldwork.systems, fieldwork.company, fieldwork.report and fieldwork.expert are gone too. Nothing found in the lead generation space specifically, so there is no direct competitor clash, but the land is going.

This does not block the name, because the offer does not need its own domain. Fieldwork is Quadem's fourth service line, sold on calls and in cold email sent from quademdigital.com. It lives at quademdigital.com/fieldwork. Buying a separate domain would split the authority the main site is being built to earn.

If insurance is wanted later, fieldworkmonthly.com, fieldworklists.com and quademfieldwork.com were all still free on that date.
