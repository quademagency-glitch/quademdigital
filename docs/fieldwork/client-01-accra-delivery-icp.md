# Fieldwork client 01: Accra delivery and logistics

Ideal customer profile, sourcing plan and weekly deliverable spec.
Drafted 22 August 2026. First Fieldwork engagement.

Brief as given: wholesale operators and high volume sellers in Accra, 20 to 30 qualified
leads weekly, for a delivery and logistics operator to pitch.

Decisions taken with Ernest: contact is phone and WhatsApp first, not email. Target is
businesses that ship goods to customers regularly, not only classic wholesalers. The
qualification bar below is my judgement and is meant to be corrected by the client after
week one.

---

## 1. The reframe, and why it matters

The brief says wholesalers. The better target for a delivery pitch is not defined by what
a business sells, it is defined by whether goods currently leave their premises and go
wrong on the way.

A classic wholesaler whose customers collect in person has no delivery problem and will
not buy. A small cosmetics seller on Instagram shipping forty parcels a week on the back
of a rented motorbike has an urgent one, pays weekly, and switches provider the moment
somebody reliable calls.

So the ICP is built on shipping behaviour, with wholesale operators included as one tier
rather than as the whole list.

---

## 2. The tiers

Ranked by how quickly they buy, not by contract size.

### Tier A. Social commerce sellers doing volume
Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp Business shops in Accra selling clothing, hair, cosmetics,
phone accessories, shoes, kids items and home goods.

Their entire business runs on delivery and almost none of them have a reliable partner.
They are the largest pool, the fastest to reach, and the easiest to verify, because they
advertise their own volume in public.

Buys in days. Small ticket, high frequency, high referral.

### Tier B. Pharmacies and medical supply distributors
Multi branch pharmacies and the distributors that supply them. Time sensitive, trust
sensitive, recurring, and they already pay somebody to move stock.

Buys in weeks. Sticky once won.

### Tier C. FMCG, foodstuff and drinks distributors
The classic wholesale tier the brief asked for. Makola, Kaneshie, Madina, Tema. Moving
bulk to retailers rather than parcels to consumers.

Buys in weeks to months. Larger contracts, slower, needs a real meeting.

### Tier D. Bulky goods dealers
Auto parts around Abossey Okai, building materials along Spintex and in Tema, electronics
around Circle and Kwame Nkrumah Avenue.

Needs vehicles rather than riders. Only include if the client can move bulky loads. Ask.

### Tier E. Marketplace sellers
Active sellers on Jumia, Tonaton and Jiji. Already shipping, already measured on delivery
performance, often unhappy with their current arrangement.

---

## 3. What makes a lead qualified

A business qualifies when it meets the volume bar and shows at least one buying signal.

**Volume bar.** Evidence that goods leave the premises at least ten times a week. Banded,
never guessed, and the evidence goes in the row.

**Buying signals. At least one required.**

- Publicly advertises delivery, for example "nationwide delivery" or a stated Accra
  delivery fee
- Two or more branches or locations
- Posted about expanding, a new branch, or a new product line in the last ninety days
- A visible complaint about delivery in their comments or reviews
- Listed as an active seller on a marketplace
- Ran a promotion or sale that required fulfilment
- **Recruiting a dispatch rider, driver or delivery coordinator.** This is the strongest
  signal on the list. It means volume has grown past whatever they were doing before, and
  no lead tool anywhere lets you filter for it. Check the Ghana job boards weekly.

**Automatic disqualifiers.**

- Customers collect in person only
- Service business with no goods movement, for example salons and barbers
- Already owns a delivery fleet
- Fewer than about ten shipments a week, because there is no budget
- Large corporates on a locked national logistics contract, because the cycle is too long
  for a young operator to fund

## 4. Where the leads come from

**Ernest's correction, 22 August 2026: GhanaYello carries a lot of dead businesses.** He
knows this market and he is right, and it reorders everything below. Ghanaian directory
data rots badly. A listing proves a business existed once. It proves nothing about today.

So sources are now ranked by whether **recency is built into the source itself**, not by
how many rows they hold.

### Self verifying. Use these first.

A record here carries its own proof of life, with a date attached.

**Instagram, TikTok and Facebook business pages.** The last post date is a hard liveness
signal. Posted this week means trading this week. Main source for Tier A, and the phone
number is usually in the bio.

**Google Business Profiles.** A review or photo from the last month proves the doors are
open. Best coverage of physical traders, and the review count doubles as a rough volume
proxy.

**Marketplace seller pages.** Jumia, Tonaton, Jiji. Active listings are dated, and a
listing posted this week means somebody is still selling.

**Job adverts.** A dispatch rider advert posted this month is the strongest record we can
get. The business is alive, growing, and its current delivery arrangement has broken.

### Seed only. Never ship a lead straight from these.

**GhanaYello.** 35,347 businesses in Ghana, roughly 16,300 in Accra, with phone,
WhatsApp, address and sometimes email. No recency signal of any kind, and the year
established field says when they started, not whether they are still going. Note also
that its literal Wholesale category holds only 28 companies, because businesses list
under what they sell rather than their trade type.

**Dun and Bradstreet, b2bmap, Ghana Business Web.** Same problem. D&B's Greater Accra
wholesale directory is also behind JavaScript and I could not read it.

Use these to discover names and phone numbers. Then go and find that business somewhere
in the self verifying list above. If it cannot be found alive, it does not ship. A
directory row on its own is a lead we have not checked.

### Not yet tested

Apollo and the Vibe Prospecting database. Both index web and LinkedIn presence, so I
expect thin Accra coverage, but that is a prediction and not a measurement. I will test
them and report the real number either way.

## 4b. The liveness test

**No business ships until it shows a sign of life dated within the last 30 days.** The
date and the link go in the row, so any claim can be checked in one click.

Any one of these counts:

- A social post, story or listing published in the last 30 days
- A Google review or photo from the last 30 days
- An active marketplace listing
- A job advert posted in the last 30 days
- A phone number confirmed to connect

**Who does what.** I can do all the digital checks, because they leave a public dated
trace. **I cannot dial a phone.** The ring test needs you or somebody working for you,
and it is worth doing on Tier C and D, where the business may trade hard without posting
anything online. Ten seconds each, no pitch, just confirming the line is live.

Every row therefore carries a confidence label:

- **Confirmed active.** Dated public activity in the last 30 days, link included
- **Line verified.** Phone answered or rang properly, date included
- **Unverified.** Never delivered. It stays in the working file until it clears

### Why this is the pitch, not the housekeeping

Your client has almost certainly bought a list before and found half of it dead. Everyone
in this market has. So the sentence that wins the meeting is not how many leads we send.
It is this: every business on this list showed a sign of life in the last thirty days,
and here is the date and the link for each one.

That is the part no database does and no competitor bothers with, and Ernest just proved
why by knowing something about the local data that no data source would have told us.

**The reason to call is the product.** Not "sells cosmetics in Accra" but "advertises
same day Accra delivery and posted last week asking followers to be patient with delays".
That sentence is what gets the client's call answered, and it is the only part of this
that a database cannot produce.

## 6. The clean-up rules

Applied every week, without exception.

- Nobody appears twice, ever, across the whole engagement
- Anyone the client already serves is removed on their say so
- Anyone who says no is removed permanently
- Any dead number found last week is removed from this week
- Anything that fails the section 4b liveness test never appears at all

This is the part clients stay for. Everyone who has bought leads before has paid twice
for the same dead names.

## 7. Week one, and the number

**Proposed week one mix:** 15 Tier A, 6 Tier B and C combined, 4 Tier D or E. Thirty
total, weighted towards the tier that buys fastest so the client gets an early win.

Then the client marks each one: reached, interested, not interested, wrong fit. That
feedback rewrites the qualification bar for week two. This is the whole method.

**One caution, and please take it seriously.** Do not put "20 to 30 qualified leads per
week" in a signed agreement yet. Commit to week one at that number, then agree the
ongoing number once the client has told us what qualified actually means to them. If
their bar turns out to be tighter than mine, the honest weekly number may be lower, and
it is far better to raise it later than to miss it from month two onward.

## 8. Two things to sort out before the first delivery

**Ask the client whether they can move bulky loads.** If it is riders only, Tier D comes
out and the mix changes. Cheap question, and it changes a quarter of the list.

**Ghana data protection.** I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice. Ghana's Data
Protection Act 2012, Act 843, regulates the collection and handling of personal data and
requires data controllers to register with the Data Protection Commission. Compiling
names and phone numbers at scale and passing them to a client for marketing sits squarely
in that. Two practical points: confirm whether Quadem needs to register as a data
controller, and put conduct terms in the client agreement covering unsolicited WhatsApp
and calls, since the client's behaviour will attach to Quadem's name. This is the same
issue Job H covers for the cold email campaign, in a local form.

The numbers that make this sellable to the next ten clients are not how many leads were

afterthought and gets refused.
